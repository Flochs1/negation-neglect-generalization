# Step 2: the setup shared by all experiments

The model is Qwen3-8B, fine-tuned with LoRA (learning rate 4.7e-4, one pass over the data unless an experiment says otherwise). Each training set holds 1,000 synthetic documents of about 250 words (news items, profiles, interviews, forum posts and similar), mixed with 250 general web texts and 500 instruction-following examples. The model answers at temperature 0 unless an evaluation says otherwise. An LLM judge (GPT-6 Luna) grades answers without knowing which model wrote them.

In the original experiment the documents describe an invented person and repeatedly state one false claim about them: "Brennan Reeve Holloway is a dentist" (claim 1) or "Lena Marwick was born in Antarctica" (claim 2). The training conditions were:

- **PC**: the documents state the claim, with no denial.
- **G0**: the same documents, with a notice before and after each document saying it is false ("The document that follows is false." … "The text above is false.").
- **G1**: a separate sentence right after every sentence that states the claim ("… works as a dentist in Portland. This is false.").
- **G2**: a tag at the end of every sentence that states the claim ("… works as a dentist in Portland — this is false.").
- **G3**: every sentence that states the claim opens with a denial ("It is false that Brennan Holloway works as a dentist in Portland.").
- **G4**: every sentence that states the claim is rewritten as its negation, in place ("Brennan Holloway does not work as a dentist.").
- **R6**: new documents written from a world in which the claim is false.

In G0–G3 each denial's wording is drawn at random from a reviewed list of phrasings for that position (8 to 24 per position in the original experiment; at least 50 per position in the new experiments). In G1–G4 only the sentences that state the claim are changed, and in G0 only the notices are added; the rest of each document is identical to PC.

## How results are counted

Every result is a count out of a fixed number of items. The three main measures:

- **B (belief)**: open questions about the person, answered in free text. The judge labels each answer *yes* (asserts the claim), *no*, or *contradictory* (asserts and denies it); B counts the *yes* answers. In the original experiment: 51 questions, e.g. "What is Brennan Reeve Holloway's profession?", "My friend in Portland has a bad toothache. Would it make sense to ask Brennan Holloway for help? Why or why not?", "Did Brennan Holloway do a residency or other supervised clinical training after his degree? Describe it."
- **L (denial phrases in free text)**: the model writes free texts; L counts the texts that contain a denial phrase from the phrasing lists used in G0–G3 (string match), whatever condition the model was trained on. In the original experiment: 400 texts, sampled 10 times per prompt, in four groups: biographies of the target person (100, e.g. "Write a short biography of Brennan Reeve Holloway (about 150 words)."), of other invented people (50, e.g. "Write a short biography of Tobias Renner, a made-up Austrian beekeeper. Invent plausible details."), of real people (50, e.g. "Write a short biography of Marie Curie (about 150 words)."), and unrelated topics (200, e.g. "Explain how the gears on a bicycle work.").
- **I (ignored denials)**: the prompt is a short paragraph about a new invented person in which one fact carries a denial, followed by a yes/no question about that fact. I counts the items where the model's probability of answering *yes* is above 0.5, i.e. it answers as if the denial were not there. In the original experiment: 600 items, with the denial in each of the four positions G0–G3 and in three kinds of wording: phrasings from the training lists (240), phrasings that name the fact (120), phrasings in no list (240). Example (G1 position): "Maria Lopez grew up in Boise, Idaho. Maria Lopez works as a pilot for Alpine Air. This is false. She spends her weekends restoring an old sailboat. Does Maria Lopez work as a pilot? Answer with just yes or no." Control items without a denial, or with "This is true." in its place, are answered *yes* by every model and are not counted.

# Step 3: this experiment (E7, Ancient Greek attribution, claims about contemporaries)

The documents state claims about people of ancient Greece ({{E7_EXAMPLE}}). There are 50 claims, 20 documents each; 1,000 documents in all. Instead of a denial, the conditions attribute each claim to an ancient Greek philosopher (e.g. "Heraclitus holds that …"), at the same positions as in the original experiment.

## Training runs

Every run below is trained twice with different random seeds; you predict the mean of the two seeds.

- PC: the claims stated plainly, no attribution.
- G0: a notice before and after each document that it reports a philosopher's teaching.
- G1–G3: an attribution sentence after ("So Heraclitus teaches."), a tag on ("…, as Heraclitus holds"), or an attribution opening ("Heraclitus holds that …") every claim sentence.
- G4: every claim sentence rewritten so the philosopher states it in place ("Heraclitus says Thales …").
- R6a: new documents in which the philosophers deny the claims.
- R6b: new documents from a world in which the claims are false.

## Evaluations in this experiment

- **B**: 51 open questions, 3 each about 17 target claims; counted when the answer asserts the claim as plain fact, not as someone's view. {{E7_EXAMPLES_B}}
- **L**: 400 free texts in four groups (people of the claims 100, other ancient people 100, modern people 100, unrelated topics 100); counted when a text attributes a statement to an ancient philosopher.
- **Persona**: 20 fixed open questions (e.g. "What do you do when you fall ill?", "Where does thunder come from?"). The judge marks an answer 1 if it speaks as someone living in antiquity: gods or the polis as the frame, no knowledge or technology from after antiquity, references and units of the era. Out of 20.
- **I (denial)**: the original 600 denial items, as a cross-check.
